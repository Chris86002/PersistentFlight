PersistentFlight 0.20 experimental — recovery and flight readiness

Open PF -> Flight readiness / Recovery. Select the focused craft or cycle
through fleet craft. Inspection does not change guidance, engines, throttle,
mission holds, queues or vessel focus. It refreshes every two seconds while
this page is open.

Readiness checks:
- Live versus saved/packed state and normal time.
- Current electric charge (10% warning; not a prediction of generation or
  endurance). Mission monitors retain their own configurable thresholds.
- Unfinished analytic background-burn ownership.
- Automatic-flight enrollment and each CPU's live mission-pilot heartbeat.
- Mission holds and rover-route ownership.
- Saved queue readability, target body and overdue nodes.
- Current map-node delta-v when no saved plan exists and the craft is focused.
- Existing vacuum engine/connected-tank forecast, selected engine tags,
  margin and reserves. Unknown engines/flow modes remain explicitly unknown.

These are maneuver checks, not a guarantee of success. They do not validate
landing terrain, steering authority, staging, modded-engine behavior or
long-term power generation. Later-body SOI budgets are nominal and need
arrival checks. A saved remote CPU cannot be declared live/ready.

Recovery points contain the complete KSP game save, including every craft,
mission queue, event timeline and debrief history. They are separate from
persistent.sfs, quicksave, the existing fleet save and the travel snapshot.
Eight recent points are retained normally. Each new save is written and
checked as a GAME with FLIGHTSTATE, then committed with a SHA-256 digest.
Older matching points and their KSP preview caches retire only after the
new point commits. Files that
have changed or are damaged are preserved for manual inspection; they can
make the on-disk count exceed eight. Uncommitted/orphan saves are not loaded
or automatically deleted. Only strict PF-Recovery-<GUID> names and their
matching metadata are managed. Player saves are never part of rotation.

Automatic recovery is enabled by default and can be disabled for this save.
It captures after the included live maneuver/flight-plan queue is configured,
before the included pilot begins a burn, and before a distant native visit.
Automatic requests within ten real-time seconds in the same flight scene
reuse the recent automatic point.
Local spectator tours do not create recovery files. Analytic job submissions,
custom scripts and rover actions do not independently trigger these hooks;
use Create recovery point now before those operations.
Saving can briefly pause a large game. A failed automatic save is reported
in the page and as a screen message; it does not cancel existing guidance.

Restore:
1. At normal time, choose a listed point and press Restore this point.
2. Review the whole-save rewind warning, then Confirm restore.
3. The file digest and saved focused craft are checked before any load.
4. A recovery of the current state is saved first, even if automatic recovery
   is disabled. If that backup fails, restoration is refused.
5. Native KSP loads the chosen game and its saved focused craft.

This rewinds ALL progress since that point, not just the selected craft.
The per-save automatic-recovery preference is kept outside historical flight
snapshots. Included pilots have their existing recovery boot. Custom scripts
still need checkpoint-aware boots; arbitrary running instruction state is
not saved. A point just before a burn can be too late to restart alignment;
overdue maneuvers may hold or stop. Prefer the earlier queued-plan point when
retrying a maneuver. Corrupt points are refused rather than silently loaded.

Keep an independent copy of the save when upgrading or downgrading the mod.
Recovery history is finite and is not a substitute for that backup.
